Circular Fashion Week, Leeds

Ndoki at Circular Fashion Week Leeds 2026

← Back to Journal
Published 12 June 2026
Category Event
Author Ndoki Studio

For the first time, Ndoki presented our Akwete Corset Revival project on an international runway — a moment that felt both deeply personal and celebratory. The collection, developed over eighteen months with master weavers in Eastern Nigeria, brings the heritage handwoven textile into contemporary silhouette while refusing to compromise on sustainability or cultural truth.

Circular Fashion Week Leeds 2026 was the perfect stage. The event, dedicated to zero-waste design and circular production, aligned with everything Ndoki stands for — making fashion that doesn't extract, but regenerates.

"Every seam tells a story. We didn't just make fashion — we invited the world to read it."

The corset — a garment historically loaded with contradiction — became our vehicle for storytelling. Working with tailors who train in London alongside returning to apprenticeships in Nigeria, we designed pieces that move between worlds, that refuse the false choice between heritage and modernity.

Each corset required six weeks of production: one week at the loom in Ndoki land, two weeks with our tailors in Lagos, two weeks for hand-finishing and fitting, one week for quality control. No shortcuts. No factories. No compromise.

The response was immediate. Four pieces sold within minutes of the show. More importantly, conversations began — with press, with retailers, with other makers interested in our model. A journalist from Vogue called it "a reminder that fashion can carry culture without consuming it."

For Ndoki, this moment wasn't about proving we belong on a runway. It was about proving that a different way of making is possible. That heritage and innovation aren't in tension. That women's hands and minds, compensated fairly and consulted throughout, make better work.

Next month, we're running a masterclass on corset-making and Akwete integration at Circular Fashion Week's education summit. Three of our lead weavers will travel to Leeds — for the first time — to teach designers how to work with handwoven textiles at scale without losing the integrity of the craft.

That feels like the real story here. Not that we showed up. But that we're making space for others to understand why.
